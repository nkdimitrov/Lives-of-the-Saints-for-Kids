More than 1,100 years ago, in the city of Thessaloniki in Greece, lived a family with seven children. The oldest was Methodius and the youngest was Constantine (who later took the name Cyril). Many Slavic people lived around Thessaloniki, so the boys grew up hearing both Greek and the Slavonic language.

### The boy who chose Wisdom

When Constantine was seven, he had a dream. All the girls of the city were gathered, and he was told to choose one to marry one day. He chose the most beautiful one — and her name was Sophia, which means **Wisdom**! From then on Constantine loved wisdom and learning more than anything.

He went to study in the great city of Constantinople, at school with the young emperor himself. He learned philosophy, math, astronomy, music and lots of languages. People called him "Constantine the Philosopher." His older brother Methodius was first a governor and then became a monk.

### An alphabet from God

One day a message came from Prince Rastislav of Moravia, a land of Slavic people far to the north: "Our people want to follow Christ, but we have no one to teach us in our own language. Please send us a teacher!"

The emperor said, "Constantine, you are the one to go. Take your brother Methodius with you."

But there was a big problem. "Do the Slavs have letters to write their language?" Constantine asked. They didn't! Without letters there could be no books, no written Gospel, no prayer books.

So Constantine prayed and fasted for forty days. And God helped him create brand-new letters — a whole alphabet — made to fit every sound of the Slavonic language! The very first words he wrote were from the Gospel of John: **"In the beginning was the Word."**

### The Gospel in their own language

In 863 the brothers arrived in Moravia with their helpers. They taught young men to read and write the new letters, and together they translated the Gospels, the Psalms and the church services. For the first time ever, the Slavic people heard the Gospel and prayed in church in their own language! They were overjoyed.

[[scene:moravia]]

But some clergy from the West were angry. "You can only praise God in three languages," they said, "Hebrew, Greek and Latin!" Constantine answered: "Doesn't the rain fall on everyone? Doesn't the sun shine for everyone? Don't we all breathe the same air? God wants every nation to praise Him!"

### A blessing in Rome

The brothers traveled to Rome, bringing with them the relics of St Clement, an early Pope, which Constantine had discovered. The Pope came out with candles to welcome them! He placed their Slavonic books on the altar of a great church and blessed them.

[[scene:rome]]

In Rome, Constantine became very sick. He became a monk and took the new name **Cyril**. Before he died, he said to Methodius: "Brother, we were like two oxen pulling one plow. Now I fall down at the end of the day. Don't give up our work!" He was only 42 years old.

### Methodius keeps going

Methodius was made an archbishop and went back to the Slavs. His enemies even put him in prison for two and a half years, but he never gave up. With his helpers, he translated almost the whole Bible into Slavonic!

After Methodius died, his students went to Bulgaria, where Prince Boris welcomed them. St Clement and St Naum taught thousands of students in Ohrid and Preslav. There the alphabet was made simpler and was named **Cyrillic** in honor of St Cyril. Today millions of people in Bulgaria, Serbia, Russia, Ukraine and other lands read and write with these letters.

Cyril and Methodius are called "Equal-to-the-Apostles," because like the Apostles they brought whole nations to Christ. They teach us to love learning and to share the Good News so everyone can understand it. Holy Cyril and Methodius, pray to God for us!
