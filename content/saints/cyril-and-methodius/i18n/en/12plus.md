### Two brothers from Thessaloniki

The holy brothers Methodius and Cyril were born in Thessaloniki, the city of St Demetrios, to a noble and devout family. Their father Leo was an officer in the imperial army. There were seven children; Methodius was the eldest, and Constantine — who later took the name Cyril — was the youngest. Around Thessaloniki lived many Slavs, and the boys grew up hearing the Slavonic language as well as Greek.

Methodius chose a career of service to the state. He was appointed governor of a region where Slavs lived, and he ruled it for about ten years. But the vanities of the world wearied him, and he left everything to become a monk in a monastery on Mount Olympus in Bithynia.

### The boy who chose Wisdom

Constantine showed extraordinary gifts from childhood. When he was seven years old, he had a dream: the governor of the city gathered all the maidens of Thessaloniki and told him to choose one of them as his bride. He looked at them all and chose the most beautiful, whose name was Sophia — that is, *Wisdom*. When he told his parents, they understood that God was calling him to a life of learning and holiness. From then on the boy loved Wisdom more than anything; he learned the writings of St Gregory the Theologian by heart and prayed to him for understanding.

Hearing of his talents, the court in Constantinople had him brought to the capital, where he studied together with the young emperor Michael. His teachers included Leo the Mathematician and Photius, later the great Patriarch. He mastered grammar, rhetoric, philosophy, geometry, astronomy and music, and learned many languages. For his learning he was called *Constantine the Philosopher*. He was offered a rich marriage and a high post in government, but he refused, was ordained, and became librarian of the great church of Hagia Sophia and a teacher of philosophy.

### Defender of the faith

Though young, Constantine defended the Orthodox faith in many debates: against the former patriarch John, an iconoclast, who mocked his youth and was defeated by his answers; and before the scholars of the Arab caliph, to whom he explained the faith in the Holy Trinity. For a time he withdrew to his brother Methodius on Mount Olympus, where they prayed and studied together.

Around the year 860 the emperor sent both brothers to the land of the Khazars, beyond the Black Sea. On the way, in Chersonesus in the Crimea, Constantine learned Hebrew, and he discovered the relics of the holy Pope Clement of Rome, who had died in exile there. At the Khazar court he debated with Jewish and Muslim teachers, and many were baptized.

### Letters for the Slavs

In the year 862 messengers came to Constantinople from Rastislav, the prince of Moravia, a Slavic land in the heart of Europe. "Our people have rejected paganism and keep the Christian law," the prince wrote, "but we have no teacher who can explain the true faith to us in our own language. Send us such a teacher." The emperor called Constantine and said: "No one can do this better than you, Philosopher. Go, and take your brother, the abbot Methodius, with you. You are both from Thessaloniki, and the people of Thessaloniki all speak pure Slavonic."

Constantine answered: "Even though I am weak and sick, I will go gladly — if they have letters for their language." "My grandfather, my father and many others have looked for them," said the emperor, "and have not found them." "Then who can write on water," said Constantine, "and who would want to be called a heretic?"

He went away and, according to the old custom, gave himself to prayer and fasting for forty days, together with his companions. And God revealed to him the letters. He composed an alphabet suited to every sound of the Slavonic tongue, and immediately began to translate the Gospel, beginning with the words: *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."*

### In Moravia

In 863 the brothers arrived in Moravia with their disciples and were received with great honor by Prince Rastislav. They gathered young men and taught them the new letters, and together they translated the books needed for the church services: the Gospels, the Apostle, the Psalter, and the services of the hours and the Liturgy. For the first time the Slavs heard the word of God and sang the praises of God in their own language, and they rejoiced and glorified Him.

[[scene:moravia]]

But Frankish clergy who had come into Moravia from the West were angry. They taught that God may be praised in only three languages — Hebrew, Greek and Latin — because those three had been written on the Cross of Christ. Constantine called this the "three-language heresy." In Venice, when many bishops and priests rose against him like crows against a falcon, he answered them: *"Does not the rain fall from God upon all equally? Does not the sun shine on all? Do we not all breathe the same air? How then are you not ashamed to recognize only three languages, and to want all other peoples and tribes to be blind and deaf?"* And he reminded them of the words of the Psalm: *"Let everything that has breath praise the Lord,"* and of the Apostle Paul: *"Let every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord."*

### In Rome

In the year 867 the brothers went to Rome, bringing with them the relics of St Clement. Pope Adrian II came out of the city with the clergy and people, carrying candles, to meet them and the holy relics. The Pope received the Slavonic books, laid them on the altar of the church of the Most Holy Mother of God, and blessed them. The Divine Liturgy was celebrated in Slavonic in the great churches of Rome, and several of the brothers' disciples were ordained priests and deacons.

### "We were a yoke of oxen"

In Rome Constantine fell gravely ill. Knowing that his end was near, he took the monastic schema with the new name Cyril, and for fifty days he prepared for death with prayer. Before he died he said to his brother: *"Brother, you and I were like a yoke of oxen plowing one furrow. Now I fall in the field at the end of my day. You love the holy mountain very much, but do not leave our teaching for the sake of the mountain, for through it you can be saved."* He fell asleep in the Lord on the 14th of February 869, at the age of forty-two, and was buried in the Basilica of St Clement in Rome.

### Methodius the Archbishop

Methodius was consecrated archbishop of Moravia and Pannonia, and he returned to the Slavs to continue the work. But hostile bishops seized him and kept him in prison in a cold land for two and a half years, until the Pope demanded his release. He suffered much, but never gave up. With two of his disciples he translated almost the whole Bible into Slavonic in a short time, as well as the books of church law and the lives of the Fathers.

Methodius fell asleep on the 6th of April 885 and was buried in his cathedral. His funeral was sung in Slavonic, Greek and Latin, and a great crowd of people of every rank wept for their teacher and shepherd.

### The disciples in Bulgaria

After his death, his disciples — Clement, Naum, Angelarius and others — were driven out of Moravia. They came to Bulgaria, where the holy Prince Boris received them as angels from God. St Clement and St Naum taught thousands of students at Ohrid and Preslav, and from Bulgaria the Slavonic books and services spread to Serbia and to Rus. There the alphabet was simplified, and the new letters were named *Cyrillic* in honor of St Cyril.

### Equal-to-the-Apostles

The Church calls Cyril and Methodius *Equal-to-the-Apostles*, because like the Apostles they brought whole nations to Christ. Every Slavic child who learns to read, every prayer sung in Church Slavonic, every book written in Cyrillic letters, is a fruit of their labors. In Bulgaria the 24th of May is celebrated as the Day of the Slavonic Letters. The holy brothers teach us to love wisdom, to honor every people and language, and to share the Gospel so that all may understand. Holy Cyril and Methodius, pray to God for us!
