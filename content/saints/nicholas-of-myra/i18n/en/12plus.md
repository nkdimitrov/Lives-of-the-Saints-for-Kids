### A child given in answer to prayer

Our holy father Nicholas was born in the city of Patara, in the land of Lycia on the southern coast of Asia Minor. His parents, Theophanes and Nonna, were noble, wealthy and devout. For many years they had no children, and they prayed with tears that God would give them a son, promising to dedicate him to the Lord. God heard them. When the child was born his mother, who had long been ill, was healed, and she bore no other children after him.

Even in the cradle the infant showed what kind of man he would become. At his baptism, the tradition says, he stood upright in the font for three hours without anyone holding him, as if he were already giving glory to the Holy Trinity. On Wednesdays and Fridays, the days of fasting, he would take his mother's milk only once, in the evening, after the evening prayers.

As he grew, Nicholas loved learning and quickly mastered his books, but he did not care for idle talk or the games of the marketplace. He kept away from bad company and spent long hours in church, reading the Holy Scriptures and praying. His uncle, also named Nicholas, was bishop of Patara. Seeing how his nephew was growing in holiness, he ordained him a reader and later a priest. At the ordination the bishop told the people with tears: "Brothers, I see a new sun rising over the earth. Blessed is the flock that will have him as its shepherd."

### Gold in the night

When his parents died, Nicholas inherited great riches. He did not keep them for himself but gave them away to the poor, remembering the Lord's words: *"Sell what you have and give alms."* He tried always to give in secret, so that only God would see.

In Patara there lived a man who had once been rich but had lost everything. He had three daughters who were old enough to marry, but he had no money for their dowries, and no one would marry them. In his despair he began to think of a shameful plan: to send his daughters out to earn money by sin. Nicholas learned of this and was grieved. He could not bear that the man should destroy his own soul and the souls of his children.

[[scene:gold]]

One night he took a bag of gold, went quietly to the man's house, and threw it in through the window. Then he hurried away so as not to be seen. In the morning the father found the gold. He wept for joy and thanked God, and with that money he married his eldest daughter honorably. Nicholas did the same for the second daughter. The father now longed to know who his benefactor was, and he stayed awake every night. When Nicholas came a third time and threw in the third bag of gold, the man ran after him, caught him, and fell at his feet. "If the Lord had not raised you up to help us," he said, "I would have perished with my whole house." Nicholas lifted him up and made him promise never to tell anyone, as long as he lived, who had given the gold.

### The storm at sea

Nicholas wished to see the holy places where our Lord had lived, suffered and risen, and so he sailed for Palestine. During the voyage the sky was clear, but Nicholas told the sailors that a terrible storm was coming. Soon the wind rose, black clouds covered the sky, and the waves grew so high that the sailors thought the ship would sink. They begged the saint to pray for them. Nicholas stood and prayed to the Lord, and the sea became calm.

[[scene:storm]]

During that same voyage one of the sailors climbed the mast, fell to the deck and was killed. Nicholas prayed over him, and the young man rose up alive, as if he had only been asleep.

In Jerusalem Nicholas venerated Golgotha, the Tomb of the Lord and all the holy places. One night he went to pray in a church that was locked, and the doors opened before him by themselves. He wanted to stay in the Holy Land and live in the desert as a hermit, but the Lord made known to him that he must return to his own country. So he sailed back to Lycia.

### The bishop chosen by God

At first Nicholas lived in the monastery of Holy Zion, which his uncle had founded, and he hoped to spend his life there in silence. But one day, while he prayed, he heard a voice: "Nicholas, this is not the field where you will bear fruit. Go out among the people, so that My name may be glorified through you." Nicholas obeyed. He went to the great city of Myra, where no one knew him, and lived there like a poor man, spending his days in church.

At that time John, the archbishop of all Lycia, who lived in Myra, died. The bishops of the region gathered to choose a new archbishop, but they could not agree. They prayed and fasted, asking God to show them the right man. The Lord revealed to the eldest bishop that they should stand at the doors of the church before the morning service, and that the first man to enter, whose name was Nicholas, was the one God had chosen. That night, as was his custom, Nicholas came to the church before anyone else. The old bishop stopped him and asked, "What is your name, child?" "Nicholas, the sinner, servant of your holiness," he answered. So he was led in, and in spite of his humble refusals he was made archbishop of Myra.

As bishop he did not change his way of life. He wore simple clothes, ate once a day in the evening, and gave everything he had to orphans, widows and the poor. He was gentle and approachable, a father to his flock, but fearless against wickedness.

### Prison and freedom

Under the emperors Diocletian and Maximian a fierce persecution of Christians began. Nicholas was arrested with many of his flock. He was bound, beaten and thrown into prison, where he suffered hunger and thirst for a long time. Yet he did not lose heart; he strengthened the other prisoners with his words and taught them to endure for Christ. When Saint Constantine became emperor and gave the Church peace, the prisoners were set free, and the people of Myra welcomed their shepherd home with great joy.

Burning with zeal for God, Nicholas then went through the land destroying the temples of the idols. In Myra stood a great temple of the goddess Artemis, which was full of demons. Nicholas tore it down to its foundations, and the demons fled from it with loud cries.

### The Council of Nicaea

In the year 325 the holy emperor Constantine called the bishops of the whole world to the city of Nicaea, because Arius, a priest of Alexandria, was teaching that the Son of God is not God, but a creature. Nicholas was among the three hundred and eighteen holy fathers of the Council. He defended the true faith with all his heart: that the Son is *of one essence with the Father*, true God from true God.

St Dimitri tells that when Arius spoke his blasphemies, Nicholas, burning with zeal, struck him on the cheek. The other fathers were troubled that a bishop had done such a thing, and they took away his bishop's vestments and kept him under guard. But in the night several of them saw the Lord Jesus Christ and the most holy Theotokos standing beside him: the Lord was giving him the Holy Gospel, and the Mother of God was placing the bishop's stole, the omophorion, on his shoulders. Then the fathers understood that God was pleased with his zeal, and they restored him to his place with honor. The Council confirmed the Orthodox faith in the Creed that we still sing today.

### Grain for the hungry

Some time later a great famine struck Lycia, and the people of Myra had no bread. Their shepherd grieved for them. One night he appeared in a dream to a merchant in Italy whose ship was loaded with grain. He gave him three gold coins as a pledge and told him to sail to Myra and sell his wheat there. When the merchant woke up, he found the three gold coins in his hand. Amazed, he sailed at once to Myra and sold his grain to the people. So the city was saved from hunger, and everyone gave thanks to God and to His servant Nicholas.

### The three innocent men

Once there was an uprising in the province of Phrygia, and the emperor Constantine sent three generals, Nepotian, Ursus and Herpylion, with their soldiers to restore order. Contrary winds forced their ships into the harbor near Myra. While they waited, some of the soldiers began to steal from the market and quarrel with the townspeople. Nicholas came to the harbor to make peace, and the generals bowed to him and ordered their men to stop.

Just then some townspeople ran up in tears. "Father," they cried, "while you are here, the governor Eustathius has condemned three innocent men to death! He has been bribed, and the whole city is weeping." Nicholas hurried to the place of execution with the generals following him. The three men were already kneeling with their eyes covered, and the executioner had raised his sword. Nicholas ran forward, seized the sword from the executioner's hands, and threw it to the ground. Then he untied the men and set them free.

[[scene:three-men]]

When the governor came to greet him, Nicholas turned away and rebuked him sternly for loving money more than justice. Eustathius fell at his feet, confessed his sin and begged forgiveness, and the bishop, seeing that he truly repented, forgave him. The generals watched all of this with wonder. They asked for the saint's blessing and went on to Phrygia, where they quickly ended the uprising without bloodshed.

### The generals in prison

When the generals returned to Constantinople, the emperor honored them greatly. But some envious courtiers slandered them, telling the emperor that they were plotting against him. The prefect Ablabius was bribed, and the three generals were thrown into prison and condemned to die. In their sorrow, Nepotian remembered how Nicholas had saved the three innocent men in Myra. "Let us pray to the God of Nicholas," he said, "and call on His servant to help us." They prayed with tears all night long.

That same night Nicholas appeared in a dream to the emperor Constantine. "Get up quickly," he said, "and set free the three generals who are in prison. They have been slandered and are innocent. If you do not listen to me, God will raise up a war against you." The emperor asked, "Who are you, that you enter my palace at night and speak to me like this?" "I am Nicholas, the sinful bishop of Myra," he answered. He appeared to Ablabius as well and warned him in the same way.

In the morning the emperor and the prefect told each other their dreams. They called the generals and asked them who Nicholas was. When they heard the whole story, Constantine set the three men free and sent them to Myra with rich gifts for the church — a Gospel book bound in gold, a golden censer and lamps — asking the holy bishop to pray for him and for his kingdom.

### Shepherd until the end

Nicholas lived to a great old age, a helper of all who called on him. Sailors in danger on the sea would cry out, "Holy Nicholas, help us!" — and many times he appeared on their ships and brought them safely to harbor. Having served the Church faithfully for many years, he fell asleep in the Lord around the year 345 and was buried in Myra. From his holy relics flowed a sweet-smelling myrrh that healed many of the sick.

Even after his death he did not stop helping those who called on him. St Dimitri tells, among many other wonders, of a boy named Basil who was carried off by raiders and made a cup-bearer to a foreign ruler. On the feast of St Nicholas his parents prayed for him with tears. As the boy stood with the wine cup in his hand, the saint caught him up and set him down, cup and all, at his parents' door.

In the year 1087 his relics were carried to the city of Bari in Italy, where they rest to this day. The Church remembers this on May 9, and his repose on December 6.

### The rule of faith

The Church sings of him: *"The truth of things revealed you to your flock as a rule of faith, an icon of meekness and a teacher of self-control."* St Nicholas teaches us to give in secret, to defend the weak, to hold fast to the true faith, and to trust that God hears the prayers of those who call on Him. Holy father Nicholas, pray to Christ God that our souls may be saved!
