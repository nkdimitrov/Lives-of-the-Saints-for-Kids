### A pagan priest's daughter

The holy great-martyr Marina was born in Antioch of Pisidia, a city in the mountains of Asia Minor (not the great Antioch of Syria), in the days when the Roman emperors were persecuting the Christians. Her father, called Aedesius in the ancient accounts, was a pagan priest who served the idols of the city. While Marina was still a baby her mother died, and her father gave the child to a nurse who lived in the countryside, not far from the city, to be fed and raised.

God arranged this for her salvation, for the nurse was a Christian. She brought Marina up as her own daughter, taught her the faith in the Holy Trinity, and had her learn the prayers and the Holy Scriptures. The girl often heard the nurse and her neighbors speaking about the holy martyrs who were suffering for Christ in those years, and her heart burned with love for the Lord and with the desire to be like them.

When Marina was older and her father learned that she had become a Christian, he turned away from her in anger and would no longer acknowledge her as his daughter. She did not grieve over this, but stayed with her nurse, and God became her Father.

### The governor on the road

When Marina was fifteen, she was out in the fields with the other girls of the village, keeping her nurse's sheep. At that time the governor of the province, Olybrius (also called Olymbrius), was travelling to Antioch to seek out and punish the Christians. As he passed by on the road, he saw Marina among the sheep and was struck by her beauty. He commanded his servants: "Bring that maiden to me. If she is free-born, I will take her as my wife."

[[scene:sheep]]

The soldiers seized Marina and brought her to him. Before the governor she prayed silently to Christ to help her, and when he asked about her family, her name and her faith, she answered: "I am of a free family of this land. My name is Marina, and I am a Christian." Olybrius ordered her to be kept under guard until he reached the city.

### The first trial

The next day the governor sat in judgment in Antioch and had Marina brought before him. First he tried to win her with gentle words. He praised her beauty and youth, and promised her riches and honor if she would offer sacrifice to the gods and become his wife. Marina answered that she worshipped the one true God, Who had made heaven and earth, and that she had promised herself to Christ as His bride. The gods of the pagans, she said, were lifeless idols made by men's hands.

Olybrius, enraged, ordered her to be stretched out and beaten without mercy. The people who stood around wept to see so young a girl suffer, and begged her: "Marina, have pity on yourself! Do what the governor says, and live!" But she replied: "Do not pity my body. This suffering is the salvation of my soul." When she still would not give in, she was cast into prison.

### The battle in the prison

In the prison, Marina prayed to God, asking Him to stand by her in the struggle and to let her see her enemy face to face. Suddenly the devil appeared before her in the form of a terrible dragon, with fire and smoke coming from its mouth, hissing as if to devour her. Some ancient tellings say that it even swallowed her. But Marina made the sign of the Cross, and the dragon vanished before the power of Christ.

Then the evil one appeared again in another form, dark and hideous, trying to frighten her into despair. But the maiden, strengthened by God, seized him, threw him down and set her foot upon his neck, rebuking him in the name of Christ until he begged to be let go. In the Greek tradition it is told that she struck him with a bronze hammer that lay in the cell; that is why the icons of St Marina show her holding a hammer over the conquered demon. For this the Church calls her the Vanquisher of Demons.

[[scene:dragon]]

After this a great light filled the prison. Marina saw a radiant Cross reaching up to heaven, and on it a white dove, and she heard a voice saying: "Rejoice, Marina, for you have conquered the evil one!" In that moment all the wounds of her body were healed, and she gave thanks to God with great joy.

### Fire and water

In the morning Marina was led out again before the governor and the people. When they saw that she was whole and unharmed, with her face shining, many were amazed. Olybrius, unwilling to recognize the power of God, gave orders that she should be burned with torches. But she endured, praying, and was not overcome.

Then the governor commanded that a great vessel be filled with water, and that the martyr be bound and thrown into it to drown. Marina lifted her eyes to heaven and prayed: "Lord, You Who are in heaven, let this water be for me the washing of holy baptism, and make me worthy of Your kingdom."

At once the earth shook, and a dove came down from heaven bearing a golden crown. The bonds fell from her hands, and the holy maiden stood up in the water, glorifying the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

[[scene:dove]]

When the people saw this wonder, a great multitude believed in Christ — St Dimitri gives their number as fifteen thousand. They cried out with one voice: "Great is the God of the Christians! There is no other God but He!" By the governor's order, all who confessed Christ were put to death outside the city, and they received the crown of martyrdom, washed in their own blood.

### The crown of martyrdom

Finally Olybrius condemned Marina to be beheaded with the sword. At the place of execution she asked for a little time to pray. She gave thanks to God, and asked Him to grant help to all who would honor her memory and call upon her in their need: forgiveness of sins, healing of sickness, and protection in danger. A voice from heaven told her that her prayer had been heard. Then she bowed her head, and so the holy virgin went to her Bridegroom, in about the year 304, during the persecution of Diocletian. She was fifteen years old.

Tradition says that a Christian named Theotimus, who had brought her bread and water in prison and witnessed her sufferings, wrote down the account of her struggle.

### Her relics and her memory

The holy relics of St Marina were later brought to Constantinople and kept there until the city was sacked by the crusaders in 1204. Today parts of them are honored in many places; her hand is kept at the Monastery of Vatopedi on Mount Athos. In Greece and Cyprus very many churches and chapels bear her name, and in the West she is honored as St Margaret of Antioch.

St Marina shows us that no age is too young to be faithful to Christ, and that the devil, however fearsome he may appear, flees from the sign of the Cross and from those who trust in God. Holy great-martyr Marina, pray to Christ God for us!
