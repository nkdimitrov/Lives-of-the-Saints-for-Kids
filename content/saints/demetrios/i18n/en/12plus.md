### A hidden church in a governor's house

The holy great-martyr Demetrios was born in the city of Thessaloniki, in Macedonia, in the days of the pagan emperors. His father was the Roman governor of the city, a man of high rank, and both his parents were Christians in secret. In an inner room of their house they kept a hidden chapel, with icons of Christ, of the Most Holy Theotokos and of the saints, and lamps burning before them. There they prayed, and there they begged God to give them a child.

The Lord heard them and gave them a son, Demetrios. When he was born, his parents held a great feast and gave many gifts to the poor. They had him baptized secretly by a priest, and as he grew they taught him the faith. Often his father led him into the hidden chapel and showed him the icons, explaining who each saint was and how much they had loved the Lord. So Demetrios grew in body, in learning and in the love of God.

### The governor who preached Christ

When his father died, the emperor Maximian, seeing that young Demetrios was wise, educated and of noble family, made him governor in his father's place over Thessaloniki and the whole region. The emperor charged him above all to destroy the Christians and to put to death anyone who called on the name of Christ.

Demetrios did the very opposite. He did not hide in fear, nor did he persecute anyone. Openly, before the whole city, he began to teach the people about Christ and to turn them away from the worship of idols. St Dimitri says that he became for Thessaloniki a second Apostle Paul — for it was Paul who had first preached the Gospel in that city. Many pagans came to believe through his words and his good life.

The emperor soon heard that the very governor he had appointed to fight against Christ had become His preacher. Maximian was returning from a war against the Sarmatians, and he turned aside to Thessaloniki to deal with Demetrios himself. Knowing what lay ahead, Demetrios called his faithful servant Lupus and told him to give all his gold, silver and possessions to the poor. "Divide my earthly riches among them," he said, "and let us seek the riches of heaven." Then he gave himself entirely to fasting and prayer.

### The scorpion and the angel

When the emperor arrived, Demetrios was called before him. He did not deny his faith but confessed boldly that he was a Christian. Maximian, furious, ordered him locked up in a dark underground room by the public baths.

While Demetrios was praying there, a scorpion crept toward him to sting him. He made the sign of the cross over it and said, *"You will tread upon the asp and the basilisk, and trample on the lion and the dragon,"* and the scorpion died at once. Then an angel of the Lord appeared to him in great light, bringing him a crown, and said: "Peace be with you, sufferer of Christ. Be brave and be strong."

### Nestor and the giant

Meanwhile, to amuse himself and the people, the emperor held games in the stadium. His favorite was a gladiator named Lyaeus, a giant of tremendous size and strength, who had come from a barbarian people. A high wooden platform was built, and underneath it were set sharp spears pointing upward. Lyaeus would wrestle men on the platform and throw them down onto the spears below. Many Christians were forced to fight him, and he killed them all. The emperor delighted in his giant and praised him to everyone.

Among the young men of the city was a boy named Nestor, who had come to faith through Demetrios. He grieved to see Christians killed and the emperor boasting. Nestor went to the prison where his teacher was kept and said: "Servant of God, Demetrios, I want to fight Lyaeus. Pray for me." Demetrios made the sign of the cross on his forehead and chest and said: "Go. You will defeat Lyaeus, and you will suffer for Christ."

[[scene:nestor]]

Nestor went boldly to the stadium. The emperor, seeing such a slender youth, felt sorry for him and offered him money to withdraw, but Nestor answered that he had come to fight. He climbed onto the platform, and with a loud voice cried out: *"God of Demetrios, help me!"* Then he grappled with the giant, and by the power of God threw him down from the platform onto the very spears on which he had killed so many. The crowd cried out in amazement, and the emperor sat in silent fury and shame.

### Martyrdom

Maximian ordered Nestor to be beheaded outside the city at once, and the young man died a martyr, as his teacher had foretold. Then the emperor learned that Demetrios had blessed Nestor. In his anger he sent soldiers to the prison with orders to kill Demetrios where he was. They found him at prayer and pierced him with their spears. So the holy Demetrios gave up his soul to the Lord, on the 26th of October, around the year 306.

His faithful servant Lupus was there. He took the saint's robe, soaked with his blood, and the ring from his finger, and with these he worked many healings in the city. For this he too was arrested and suffered martyrdom. The Christians buried Demetrios's body secretly, and the sick who came to his grave were healed.

### The Myrrh-streamer of Thessaloniki

Some years later a high official named Leontius fell gravely ill and could not walk. He had himself carried to the place where Demetrios was buried, and there he was healed. In thanksgiving he built a beautiful church over the saint's tomb. From the holy relics there began to flow a fragrant myrrh, which has brought healing to many, and so the Church calls St Demetrios the *Myrrh-streamer*.

[[scene:protector]]

The people of Thessaloniki have always believed that their saint watches over them. Many times, when barbarian armies besieged the city, St Demetrios was seen on the walls or riding before the gates, and the enemies fled in terror. Christians in many lands, especially the Slavs, love him as a mighty protector.

### A martyr for our time

St Demetrios teaches us that every Christian, whatever his work or position, is called to witness to Christ where he is placed — a governor in his city, a child among his friends. And he shows us, through little Nestor, that the strength of God is made perfect in weakness. Holy great-martyr Demetrios, pray to Christ God for us!
