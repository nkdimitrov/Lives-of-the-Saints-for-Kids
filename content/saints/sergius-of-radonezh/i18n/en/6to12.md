About 700 years ago, in a land of deep forests and wooden villages that we now call Russia, a boy named Bartholomew was born to a kind family. Even before he was born, something amazing happened: while his mother was in church, the baby inside her cried out three times during the Liturgy! The priest said, "This child will be a servant of the Holy Trinity."

### The boy who couldn't read

When Bartholomew was seven, he went to school with his brothers. His brothers learned to read quickly, but Bartholomew just couldn't. The letters were all jumbled up! The teacher scolded him and the other kids laughed. Bartholomew felt sad, and he prayed, "God, please help me learn to read."

One day his father sent him into the fields to find some lost horses. Under a big oak tree, Bartholomew saw an old monk praying. He waited politely until the monk finished, then told him about his problem.

[[scene:oak]]

The old monk prayed for him and gave him a small piece of holy bread. "Eat this," he said. "From now on, you will be able to read." Bartholomew invited him home, and there the elder handed him the book of Psalms. Bartholomew opened it — and read it perfectly! The elder told his parents, "Your son will be great before God," and then he disappeared. They realized he had been an angel!

### A hermit in the forest

When Bartholomew grew up and his parents had died, he went with his brother Stephen deep into the forest. On a hill they built a tiny log cabin and a little wooden church for the Holy Trinity. Life was so cold and hard that Stephen left. But Bartholomew stayed. He became a monk and got a new name: **Sergius**.

Sergius lived alone in the forest for a long time. Wolves howled around his cabin at night, but he prayed and was not afraid.

One day a big, hungry bear came out of the trees. Sergius felt sorry for it and put a piece of bread on a tree stump. The bear ate it and left — but it came back the next day, and the next! Sergius always shared his bread with it, even when he had only one piece left.

[[scene:bear]]

### The humble abbot

Other monks heard about Sergius and came to live with him. They asked him to be their abbot, their leader. Sergius didn't want to be in charge, but he finally agreed. Even as abbot, he worked harder than anyone! He chopped wood, carried water up the hill for everyone, baked bread and sewed clothes. His robe was so patched and old that visitors sometimes thought he was a servant.

Once the monastery had no food for three days. Sergius built a porch for another monk and was paid with a basket of moldy bread — and he thanked God for it! That very evening, wagons full of fresh food arrived from someone nobody knew.

When the monks complained that the water was too far away, Sergius prayed, and a spring of fresh water bubbled up right near the monastery. You can still drink from it today!

### The prince's blessing

In 1380 a huge army of Tatars came to attack Russia. The Grand Prince of Moscow, Dmitry, came to Sergius and asked for his blessing.

[[scene:blessing]]

Sergius blessed him and said, "Go, my lord, and God will help you win." He even sent two of his monks, who had been soldiers before, to go with him. The Russians won a great victory. While the battle was happening far away, Sergius prayed in church with his monks and told them what was happening, as if he could see it!

### A visit from the Mother of God

One night, while Sergius was praying, a bright light filled his room, and the Mother of God appeared with the Apostles Peter and John. "Don't be afraid," she said. "I will always take care of your monastery." And she has.

St Sergius died in 1392, when he was about 78 years old. His little wooden church in the forest grew into one of the most famous monasteries in the world. St Sergius teaches us to be humble, to work hard, to be kind to animals and to trust God for everything. Holy Sergius, pray to God for us!
