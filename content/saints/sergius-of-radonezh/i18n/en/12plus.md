### A child chosen before his birth

Our holy father Sergius was born around the year 1314 in the village of Varnitsa near the city of Rostov, to the noble and devout Cyril and Maria. Even before his birth God showed that he would be a chosen vessel. One Sunday, while his mother stood in church during the Divine Liturgy, the child in her womb cried out three times, so that everyone heard it: when the Gospel was about to be read, at the Cherubic Hymn, and when the priest proclaimed, *"Let us attend! Holy things for the holy!"* The priest told his parents: "Rejoice! This child will be a servant of the Most Holy Trinity."

He was baptized with the name Bartholomew. St Dimitri tells that on Wednesdays and Fridays the infant would not take his mother's milk, and when his mother ate meat, he would not nurse, so that she gave it up altogether.

### The boy who could not read

When Bartholomew was seven, he was sent to learn his letters together with his brothers Stephen and Peter. His brothers learned quickly, but Bartholomew could not. His teacher scolded him, his parents grieved, and the other children laughed at him. In secret he wept and prayed: "Lord, help me to learn to read."

One day his father sent him to look for some horses that had wandered off. In a field, under an oak tree, he saw an old monk standing in prayer. The boy waited respectfully until he had finished, and then came and bowed. "What do you seek, child?" the elder asked. "My soul longs above all to learn to read," said Bartholomew, "and I cannot. Holy father, pray to God for me." The elder lifted up his hands and prayed, and then took from his pouch a small piece of prosphora, the blessed bread. "Take this and eat," he said. "It is given to you as a sign of God's grace and of understanding of the Holy Scriptures. From today you will know your letters better than your brothers."

The boy begged the elder to come to his parents' house. There the old man went first into the little chapel and gave Bartholomew the Psalter, telling him to read. The boy, who had never been able to read, began to sing the psalms clearly and without a mistake. After the meal the elder told Cyril and Maria: "Your son will be great before God and men. He will be a dwelling-place of the Holy Trinity, and he will lead many to understand the will of God." Then he went out and vanished from their sight, and they understood that he had been an angel of God.

### Into the forest

Hard times came upon the family: heavy taxes to the Tatar Horde and the cruelty of officials made them poor, and they moved to the small town of Radonezh. Bartholomew grew up quiet, gentle and devoted to prayer and fasting. He wished to become a monk, but he stayed with his parents to care for them in their old age. Before they died, they both took monastic vows.

After their death Bartholomew gave his inheritance to his younger brother Peter and went with his elder brother Stephen, who had become a monk after his wife died, deep into the forest. On a hill called Makovets, near a spring, they cut down trees and built a little cell and a small wooden church, which they dedicated to the Most Holy Trinity. But the life there was very hard — wild animals, cold, hunger and loneliness — and Stephen soon left for a monastery in Moscow.

Bartholomew remained alone. An abbot named Mitrophan came and tonsured him as a monk with the name Sergius; he was twenty-three years old. For a long time he lived in complete solitude in the forest. Packs of wolves howled around his cell at night, and bears wandered by; demons tried to frighten him away. But Sergius prayed, and God protected him.

### The bear

One day a big bear came to his cell, weak with hunger. Sergius took pity on it and laid a piece of bread on a stump. The bear ate it and went away, but it came back again and again, and the saint always shared his bread with it. When he had only one piece left, he gave it to the bear and went hungry himself, saying that the bear could not fast or understand why it went without, while he could.

### The abbot who served everyone

The fame of the hermit spread, and other monks began to come to him, asking to live with him. At first there were twelve. They built cells and a fence around them. Sergius served them all like a servant bought with money: he chopped and carried wood, drew water from the spring and carried it up the hill in two buckets for every cell, baked the bread, cooked the food, sewed shoes and clothing, and stood first in church and last to leave.

The brothers begged him to become their abbot. Sergius refused for a long time, saying: "My desire is to learn, not to teach; to obey, not to command." At last, in obedience to the bishop, he was ordained a priest and made abbot. But he did not change his way of life: his robe was so old and patched that visitors sometimes mistook him for a laborer.

Once there was no bread in the monastery for three days. Sergius went to an elder named Daniel, who wanted a porch built onto his cell, and offered to build it for him. Daniel had only a basket of moldy bread to pay him. Sergius worked all day, built the porch, and received the basket gratefully. That very evening carts loaded with fresh bread, fish and other food arrived at the monastery from an unknown benefactor.

When the brothers complained that the water was far away, Sergius went with one monk into a ravine below the monastery, prayed, and a spring gushed out there which flows to this day. Through his prayers many were healed, and he even raised a dead child whose father had carried him to the monastery.

### The Patriarch's cross and the Metropolitan's gold

Messengers came from the Patriarch of Constantinople with a cross and a letter, blessing Sergius to establish the common life in his monastery, with everything held in common and the brothers eating together. Sergius did so, and many other monasteries were founded by his disciples throughout Russia.

The holy Metropolitan Alexis of Moscow loved Sergius and wished him to become Metropolitan after him. He offered him a golden cross adorned with jewels. Sergius bowed and said: "Forgive me, Master, but from my youth I have not been a wearer of gold, and in my old age I wish all the more to remain in poverty." And he could not be persuaded.

When princes quarreled, Sergius walked on foot to their cities and made peace between them. He always went on foot, even on long journeys.

### The blessing before Kulikovo

In 1380 the Tatar commander Mamai marched against Russia with a huge army. Grand Prince Dmitry of Moscow came to the Holy Trinity Monastery to ask the saint's blessing. Sergius served the Liturgy, blessed him with holy water, and said: *"Go against the godless, my lord, and with God's help you will be victorious and return home in health."* At the prince's request he gave him two of his monks, Alexander Peresvet and Andrew Oslyabya, who had been warriors before they became monks, and he blessed them with the great schema instead of armor.

On the 8th of September, the feast of the Nativity of the Theotokos, the armies met on the field of Kulikovo, and the Russians won a great victory. During the battle Sergius stood in prayer with the brothers in the church, and he told them what was happening as if he saw it, naming the warriors who had fallen and praying for them.

### The visit of the Mother of God

One night, as Sergius was singing the Akathist before the icon of the Mother of God, he said to his disciple Micah: "Be watchful, child, for we shall have a wondrous visitor." Suddenly a dazzling light filled the cell, and the Most Holy Theotokos appeared with the Apostles Peter and John. She said: "Do not be afraid, my chosen one. Your prayers for your disciples have been heard. I will not abandon this place." Micah fell down as if dead from fear, but Sergius was filled with joy.

Six months before his death, Sergius handed over the leadership of the monastery to his disciple Nikon. He fell asleep in the Lord on the 25th of September 1392, about seventy-eight years old. Thirty years later his relics were found incorrupt, on July 5, 1422.

### The lamp of Russia

The Holy Trinity–St Sergius Lavra, which grew from his little wooden church in the forest, has been a heart of Orthodox Russia for more than six hundred years. St Sergius teaches us humility, hard work, love for God's creatures, peacemaking and trust in God's providence. Holy father Sergius, pray to God for us!
