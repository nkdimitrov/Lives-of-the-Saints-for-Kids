About 1,700 years ago, in a city called Caesarea in Cappadocia, a boy named Basil was born into a very special family. His grandmother, his mother, his father, his big sister and two of his brothers all became saints! Imagine a whole family of saints around the dinner table.

Basil loved to learn. He studied with the best teachers in his city, then in the great capital, Constantinople, and finally in Athens, the most famous city of learning in the world.

### Best friends in Athens

In Athens Basil met a young man named Gregory, and they became best friends for life. They were so close that people said they had one soul in two bodies. While other students went to parties, Basil and Gregory studied hard and prayed. Gregory later wrote that they knew only two roads in Athens: the road to church and the road to school.

[[scene:athens]]

Basil became one of the cleverest men of his time. But he realized something important: all the knowledge in the world is not worth much if you do not know God. So he traveled to Egypt and the Holy Land to learn from holy monks, and he was baptized in the river Jordan, where Jesus Himself was baptized. People say a bright light shone around him and a dove flew up from the water!

### A soup kitchen for everyone

Basil gave away his money and became a monk, living a simple life of prayer and work in the mountains with his friend Gregory. Later the bishop of Caesarea asked him to come back and help, and Basil became a priest.

Then a terrible thing happened. For a whole year it didn't rain. The crops dried up, and people began to starve. Some rich people hid their grain to sell it for lots of money. Basil was upset. "The bread you keep belongs to the hungry," he preached. "The coat in your closet belongs to the one who has no coat!"

[[scene:famine]]

Basil sold everything he had left and bought food. He set up huge pots of soup and fed anyone who was hungry, Christian or not. He put on an apron and served people himself! Later he built a whole "city of mercy" with a hospital, a home for the poor and a place for travelers. It was one of the first hospitals in the world.

### "You've never met a bishop!"

Basil became the archbishop of Caesarea. In those days the emperor, Valens, followed a false teaching that said Jesus was not truly God. He wanted all the bishops to agree with him, so he sent a scary official named Modestus to frighten Basil.

"I can take away all your things!" shouted Modestus. "I can send you away! I can hurt you, even kill you!"

Basil answered calmly: "I don't own anything except some old clothes and a few books. The whole earth belongs to God, so wherever you send me, I'll be at home. And if I die, I'll just be with God sooner!"

[[scene:modestus]]

Modestus was amazed. "No one has ever talked to me like that!" he said. "Maybe," Basil replied, "you've never met a real bishop before."

The emperor decided to send Basil away anyway. But when he tried to sign the order, his pen broke. He tried again, and the second pen broke. The third pen broke too! The emperor gave up.

### St Basil's bread

There is a lovely tradition about Basil. Once, the people of Caesarea had collected their gold to pay off a cruel emperor, but in the end the city was saved. Basil wanted to give the gold back, but no one knew whose coins were whose. So — the story says — he baked the coins into small loaves of bread and gave them out, and everyone found their treasure! That is why on January 1, St Basil's Day, many Orthodox families bake a special bread called the *Vasilopita* with a coin hidden inside.

St Basil wrote many books and prayers, including the Divine Liturgy of St Basil, which we still pray in church. He died on January 1, when he was only 49 years old. St Basil teaches us to love learning, to share with the hungry, and to be brave for God. Holy Basil, pray to God for us!
