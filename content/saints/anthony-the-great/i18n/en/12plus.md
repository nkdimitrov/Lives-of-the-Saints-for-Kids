### A boy who loved the house of God

Our holy father Anthony was born in Egypt, in the village of Koma near the river Nile, around the year 251. His parents were Christians, well-born and wealthy, and they raised him in the fear of God. As a child he did not want to go to school with the other boys, for he did not wish to pick up their bad habits, but he loved to go to church with his parents. He listened carefully to the readings and kept them in his heart. He was obedient, content with simple food, and never begged his parents for delicacies.

### "Go, sell what you possess"

When Anthony was about eighteen or twenty years old, his parents died, leaving him with a younger sister and a large estate. Not six months later, as he was walking to church, he was thinking about how the Apostles had left everything to follow the Saviour, and how the first Christians in Jerusalem sold their houses and laid the money at the Apostles' feet. He entered the church just as the Gospel was being read, and he heard the Lord's words to the rich young man: *"If you would be perfect, go, sell what you possess and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow Me."*

Anthony felt that these words had been read for him. He went straight out of the church and gave the people of his village his land — some three hundred fertile acres — so that neither he nor his sister would be troubled by it. He sold all his other possessions and gave the money to the poor, keeping only a little for his sister. But when he went to church again and heard the Lord say, *"Do not be anxious about tomorrow,"* he gave away even that. He entrusted his sister to a community of devout virgins to be raised, and he himself began to live a life of prayer and labor near his village.

### Learning from everyone

In those days there were not yet monasteries in the desert. Those who wished to live for God alone dwelt on the edges of their villages. Anthony went to these elders like a wise bee, gathering from each the honey of virtue: from one he learned kindness, from another constant prayer, from another fasting, from another patience and gentleness. He worked with his hands, giving part of his earnings to the poor and buying bread with the rest. He prayed without ceasing, for he had heard that we must pray always, and he listened so well to the Scriptures that nothing he heard was lost; his memory took the place of books.

### The struggle with the enemy

The devil, who hates goodness, could not bear to see such zeal in a young man. First he troubled Anthony with thoughts: memories of his wealth, worry for his sister, the pleasures of food and comfort, and how long and hard the road of virtue was. But Anthony drove these away with faith and prayer. Then the enemy troubled him with impure thoughts and fantasies, but Anthony fought them with fasting and vigils, eating only bread and salt with water once a day after sunset, and often staying awake all night in prayer.

Then Anthony went further away, to some old tombs outside the village, and shut himself inside one of them, asking a friend to bring him bread from time to time. There the demons attacked him openly. One night they beat him so severely that he lay speechless, and his friend, finding him in the morning, carried him to the village church. But as soon as Anthony came to himself, he asked to be taken back to the tomb.

There, at night, the demons made such a noise that the place seemed to shake. They appeared in the shapes of wild beasts — lions, bears, leopards, bulls, serpents, scorpions and wolves — each roaring and rushing at him. Anthony, though in pain, mocked them: "If you had any power, one of you would be enough. But since the Lord has taken away your strength, you try to frighten me with your numbers. Faith in our Lord is a seal and a wall of protection for us."

Suddenly the roof seemed to open, and a beam of light came down upon him. The demons vanished, his pain left him, and the place was whole again. Anthony understood that the Lord had come to help him, and he said: "Where were you, my good Jesus? Why did You not come at the beginning to end my pains?" And a voice answered: *"Anthony, I was here, but I waited to see your struggle. Since you have endured and not been defeated, I will always be your helper, and I will make your name known everywhere."* Anthony was then thirty-five years old.

### The desert becomes a city

Now Anthony went out into the desert itself. On the way the enemy placed in his path a large silver dish and then a heap of gold, but Anthony passed them by as if they were fire. He crossed the river and found an abandoned fort on a mountain called Pispir, full of snakes, which fled before him. There he shut himself in, and for twenty years he lived alone, receiving bread through the roof twice a year.

Many people longed to see him and to imitate his life. At last his friends broke down the door. Anthony came out as if from a holy sanctuary, filled with the Spirit of God. Those who saw him were amazed: his body was neither weakened by fasting nor fattened, his soul was calm and his face peaceful, neither sad nor overly merry. Through him the Lord healed many of the sick and set free those troubled by demons. He comforted the sorrowful and reconciled enemies, urging everyone to prefer nothing in the world to the love of Christ.

Many were persuaded by his words to take up the monastic life, and monasteries sprang up in the mountains. St Athanasius writes that the desert became a city, filled with monks who had left their own homes and enrolled themselves as citizens of heaven.

When the emperor Maximinus persecuted the Church in Alexandria, Anthony went into the city to strengthen the confessors in the prisons and the mines and to stand by the martyrs at their trials. He longed to suffer for Christ himself, but the Lord kept him to be a teacher of many.

### The Inner Mountain

When the crowds grew too great, Anthony went deep into the desert with some Bedouin travelers, to a high mountain near the Red Sea. At its foot was a spring of clear water and a few palm trees. He loved the place and stayed there. To avoid burdening anyone, he dug a little garden and grew grain and vegetables. When wild animals came to drink and trampled his plants, he gently caught one of them and said to them all: "Why do you harm me, when I do not harm you? In the name of the Lord, do not come near this place." And from that day they did not come.

### Bread from heaven

St Dimitri tells, from the Life of St Paul of Thebes, that when Anthony was about ninety years old, the thought came to him that no monk had lived in the desert longer than he. But it was revealed to him that there was one deeper in the desert who was far holier than he. Anthony set out at once and, after a long search, came to a cave where the aged Paul had lived alone for nearly a century.

The two old men greeted each other by name, though they had never met, and embraced. As they sat talking about the things of God, a raven flew down and gently set a whole loaf of bread before them. "See," said Paul, "the Lord has sent us our dinner. For sixty years I have received half a loaf every day; but now that you have come, Christ has doubled the portion for His soldiers."

Soon afterwards Paul fell asleep in the Lord, and Anthony found him kneeling with his hands lifted up, as if still praying. Having no spade, Anthony did not know how to bury him, but two lions came out of the desert, dug a grave with their paws and went away. Anthony buried his brother and kept Paul's tunic, woven from palm leaves, which he wore on the great feasts of Pascha and Pentecost.

### The wisdom of the unlettered

Anthony had never learned to read and write, yet the wise came to him and went away amazed. Once two pagan philosophers came to test him. "Tell me," Anthony asked them, "which comes first, the mind or letters?" "The mind," they said. "Then the one whose mind is healthy has no need of letters," he answered. And to another he said: "My book is the nature of created things, and it is always before me when I wish to read the words of God."

When the Arians spread their heresy, St Athanasius asked him to come to Alexandria, and the old man came down from the mountain and told the people that Christ is true God, of one essence with the Father. The emperor Constantine himself and his sons wrote to Anthony as to a father, asking for his prayers.

### A peaceful end

At the age of one hundred and five, knowing that his end was near, Anthony gathered his disciples and told them: "Watch and pray, keep yourselves from evil thoughts, and live each day as if you were dying that day. Hold fast to the true faith." He asked them to bury his body secretly, so that no one would honor it with excessive pomp. He gave one of his sheepskins to St Athanasius and the other to Bishop Serapion, and his hair shirt to his disciples. Then, with a joyful face, he fell asleep in the Lord in the year 356.

St Anthony is called the Father of Monks, for from him the monastic life spread through the whole world. He teaches us to listen to the Gospel as if Christ were speaking to us, to fight the enemy with prayer and humility, and to fear nothing but separation from God. Holy father Anthony, pray to Christ God for us!
