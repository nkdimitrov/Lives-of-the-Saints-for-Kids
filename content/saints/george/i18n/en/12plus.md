### A soldier from Cappadocia

The holy great-martyr George was born in Cappadocia, in Asia Minor, to Christian parents who were rich and devout. While he was still a boy his father suffered a martyr's death for Christ. His mother then took him to her own homeland, Palestine, where she owned large estates near the town of Lydda, and there she raised him in faith and in the fear of God.

George grew up tall, strong and handsome, and he was brave in body and in soul. He entered the army of the Roman Empire and soon stood out among all the soldiers for his courage and wisdom in battle. While still a young man he was made a tribune, a commander of a thousand men, and the emperor Diocletian, who did not know that he was a Christian, made him one of his own trusted officers. When George was about twenty, his mother died, and he inherited a great fortune.

### "I am a Christian"

At that time Diocletian decided to wipe out the Christian faith completely. He gathered his council in the city of Nicomedia, and together they resolved that every Christian in the empire must either sacrifice to the idols or be put to death. George heard of this decision. He understood that the hour had come for him to confess Christ openly, and he prepared himself like a soldier going into battle. He gave his gold, silver and clothing to the poor, set free all his servants, and went to the palace.

[[scene:confession]]

There, in front of the emperor and all the nobles, George stood up and said in a loud voice: "How long, O emperor and you rulers, will you rage against the Christians? Why do you make unjust laws against those who have done no wrong? Your idols are not gods. Our Lord Jesus Christ alone is the true God. Come to know the truth, or at least do not trouble those who know it."

Everyone fell silent, astonished that anyone would dare to speak like this. The emperor asked his consul Magnentius to answer him, and the consul asked George who had given him such boldness. "The truth," said George. "And what is the truth?" asked the consul. "The truth is Christ Himself, whom you persecute," George answered. "I am His servant, a Christian, and I stand here to bear witness to the truth."

Diocletian, who had admired George, tried at first to win him back with kind words. He promised him gold and the highest honors if he would only offer a little incense to the gods. But George replied: "All the riches and honors of your kingdom are nothing compared to the love of Christ. You cannot turn me away from Him." Then the emperor in his anger ordered his guards to drive George out with their spears and throw him into prison. As they pushed him, a spear that touched his body bent as if it were made of soft lead.

### Trials and wonders

In the prison the guards fastened George's feet in wooden stocks and laid a heavy stone on his chest. He suffered all night, yet he gave thanks to God. The next day he was brought before the emperor again. "Have you changed your mind?" Diocletian asked. "Do you think," said George, "that this little stone has made me weak? You will grow tired of tormenting me sooner than I will grow tired of suffering for Christ."

Then the emperor ordered a great wheel to be made, with sharp blades set beneath it, and George was bound to it and turned. As he suffered he prayed quietly, and at last he lay still, and everyone thought he was dead. The emperor went off to the temple to thank his gods. But a voice was heard from heaven: *"Do not fear, George, for I am with you,"* and an angel in shining white appeared and healed all his wounds.

[[scene:angel]]

When George stood up whole and came back to the emperor, many who saw it were amazed. Two officers, Anatolius and Protoleon, declared that they believed in Christ and were put to death at once. Even the empress Alexandra began to believe in her heart.

Diocletian would not be moved. He had George thrown into a pit of quicklime, but after three days he was found unharmed, praising God. He had him put in iron boots with nails, and the next morning George walked in healed. The emperor's sorcerer Athanasius mixed poison into two cups and gave them to George to drink, but he made the sign of the cross over them and drank without harm. Then the sorcerer said, "Let us see if your God can raise the dead." George prayed, the earth shook, and a man who had long been dead came out of his tomb alive. Athanasius fell at George's feet and confessed Christ, and he too was put to death for the faith.

A poor farmer named Glycerius, whose only ox had died, came to the prison and begged George to pray for him. George told him, "Go, brother, your ox is alive," and so it was. Glycerius ran through the city shouting that the God of the Christians is the true God, and he received a martyr's crown. Many others came secretly to the prison to be healed and taught, and George baptized them.

### The idols fall

At last the emperor tried a new trick. He spoke gently to George and invited him to the temple of Apollo, hoping that he would offer sacrifice in front of everyone. George agreed to go. When he stood before the statue of Apollo, he asked it: "Do you wish me to offer sacrifice to you as to God?" The demon who lived in the idol cried out: "I am not God, and none of us is God. The only God is the One you preach." At that moment George made the sign of the cross, and all the idols in the temple fell from their places and were shattered.

The priests and the crowd rushed at George in fury. When the empress Alexandra heard what had happened, she came through the crowd and fell at his feet, openly confessing that she was a Christian. The emperor condemned them both to death. As Alexandra was led out, she sat down by a wall and quietly gave her soul to God. George went joyfully to the place of execution, praying for himself and for his persecutors, and he was beheaded on the 23rd of April in the year 303. His body was carried to Lydda and laid in a church built in his honor.

### The miracle of the serpent

The Church also tells of a great wonder that St George worked after his death. Near the city of Lasia, ruled by a king who worshipped idols, a terrible serpent lived in a lake and attacked the people. To keep it away, the townspeople began to give it their children, drawing lots for whose child would be next. At last the lot fell on the king's own daughter. The king wept, but she was dressed in royal clothes and led out to the lakeshore.

[[scene:dragon]]

As she stood weeping, a young warrior in shining armor rode up on a white horse. "Do not be afraid," he said. "In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ I will deliver you." When the serpent rose from the water, the rider made the sign of the cross, called on Christ, and struck it with his spear, pinning it to the ground. Then he told the princess to tie her sash around the beast's neck, and she led it, as gentle as a lamb, into the city. The people were terrified, but the rider said, "Do not fear. Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, who sent me to save you." Then he put the serpent to death. The king and all his people believed and were baptized, and they built a church in honor of the Most Holy Theotokos and of the holy great-martyr George.

This is why St George is shown on icons riding a white horse and slaying the dragon: he is a picture of Christ's victory over evil, and of the help the saint gives to all who call on him.

### Trophy-bearer

The Church calls St George "the Trophy-bearer", because he won the victory over the enemy of our salvation. He is a protector of soldiers, a friend of farmers and shepherds, and a swift helper of the poor and of captives. Holy great-martyr George, pray to Christ God that our souls may be saved!
