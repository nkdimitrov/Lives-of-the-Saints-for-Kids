### Abba Zosimas goes into the desert

This life was written down by St Sophronius, Patriarch of Jerusalem, from the account handed down by the monks who had heard it from Abba Zosimas himself.

In a monastery in Palestine there lived a priest-monk named Zosimas, who had served God from his childhood with fasting, prayer and the study of the Scriptures. When he was fifty-three years old, the thought came to him that he had reached perfection and that no one could teach him anything more. Then an angel appeared to him and said: "Zosimas, you have struggled well, but no one has reached perfection. Leave your monastery and go to the one near the river Jordan, and you will learn how many paths there are to salvation."

Zosimas obeyed. The monastery by the Jordan had a beautiful custom. On the first Sunday of Great Lent the brothers received Holy Communion, embraced one another and then crossed the Jordan, each going alone into the desert to spend the whole fast in prayer. They returned on Palm Sunday, and no one asked another how he had spent the forty days.

Zosimas too crossed the river and walked deep into the wilderness, praying as he went. On the twentieth day, at noon, as he stood singing the psalms, he saw to his right something like a human figure. Its body was black from the sun, and its short hair was white as wool. When the figure saw him, it fled. Zosimas, forgetting his old age, ran after it, calling out: "Servant of God, wait for me, a sinner, for the sake of the Lord!"

The figure stopped across a dry streambed and called back: "Abba Zosimas, forgive me for the Lord's sake, but I cannot turn to face you. I am a woman, and I have nothing to cover me. Throw me your cloak, so that I may come to you and receive your blessing." Zosimas trembled, for she had called him by his name, though she had never seen him. He threw her his cloak, and she wrapped herself in it and came to him.

### "I was a sinner"

Each asked the other for a blessing, and for a long time neither would give it first. At last she said: "You are a priest, Abba; it is right for you to bless me." Then she stood to pray, and Zosimas saw her lifted up from the ground about a cubit high, standing in the air as she prayed. He was afraid she might be a spirit, but she said: "Why are you troubled, Abba? I am a sinful woman, but I have been washed in holy Baptism." Zosimas fell at her feet and begged her, by Christ, to tell him who she was. And she told him her life.

"I was born in Egypt," she said. "When I was twelve years old, I rejected my parents' love and ran away to Alexandria. There I lost my innocence and for seventeen years lived a life of shameless sin. I did not do it for money — I often refused payment — but because I was burning with a desire for pleasure that was never satisfied. That was my life, and I thought it was living.

"One summer I saw a great crowd of people going down to the sea to sail to Jerusalem for the feast of the Elevation of the Precious Cross. I went with them, not for the sake of the feast but for my own amusement, and on the voyage and in the holy city I led many into sin.

[[scene:icon]]

"On the day of the feast I went with the crowd to the church to see the Cross. When I reached the doors, everyone else went in, but an invisible power held me back. I tried again and again, three or four times, but I could not cross the threshold. Then, standing in the corner of the porch, I understood that it was my sins that kept me from seeing the life-giving Wood. I began to weep and beat my breast. Looking up, I saw above me an icon of the Most Holy Theotokos. I said to her: 'O Lady, Virgin who gave birth to God in the flesh, I know that it is not right for one so unclean as I am to look upon your pure icon. But help me, for I have no one else. Let me enter and see the Cross on which your Son was crucified, and I promise you that I will never again defile myself, but will go wherever you lead me.'

"Then I felt a warmth of faith in my heart, and I went back to the doors, and now I entered without any hindrance. I saw the Precious Cross and venerated it, and I understood how ready God is to receive those who repent. Returning to the icon, I said: 'Now, Lady, lead me on the way of repentance.' And I heard a voice from far away: *'If you cross the Jordan, you will find glorious rest.'*

"As I left, a stranger gave me three coins, and with them I bought three loaves of bread. That evening I reached the church of St John the Baptist by the Jordan. I washed my face in the holy river, received the Holy Mysteries, ate half a loaf, and the next day crossed into this desert. That was forty-seven years ago."

### Forty-seven years in the desert

Zosimas asked her how she had lived. "For seventeen years," she said, "I fought with my passions as with wild beasts. I longed for the wine and the food of Egypt, and the songs I used to sing came back into my mind. Then I would weep and beat my breast, and I would remember the promise I had made before the icon of the Theotokos, and I would ask her to help me. She never abandoned me. After seventeen years a great calm came, and since then the word of God has fed me." Her clothes had long since fallen to pieces; she had suffered heat and cold; the three loaves had lasted her a long time, and then she had eaten the herbs of the desert.

Zosimas noticed that she quoted the Psalms and the Scriptures, and he asked whether she had studied them. "I have never learned to read," she said, "nor have I heard anyone sing the psalms. But the Word of God, which is living and active, teaches man knowledge."

Then she said: "I beg you, Abba, tell no one what you have heard until God takes me from this world. Next year, do not cross the Jordan with the other monks. On Great and Holy Thursday, bring the life-giving Body and Blood of Christ and wait for me on the bank of the river on this side." And she disappeared into the desert.

### Across the water

A year passed. On Holy Thursday Zosimas took the Holy Gifts in a small chalice, with a few figs, dates and a little lentils, and went to the bank of the Jordan. Late in the evening he saw her standing on the far side. How would she cross? There was no boat. But she made the sign of the Cross over the water, stepped onto it, and walked across the river as if on dry land.

[[scene:jordan]]

Zosimas wanted to bow down before her, but she cried out: "What are you doing, Abba? You are a priest and you carry the Holy Gifts!" She asked him to read the Creed and the Lord's Prayer, and then she received the Holy Mysteries. Lifting her hands, she said: *"Lord, now let Your servant depart in peace, according to Your word, for my eyes have seen Your salvation."* She asked him to come again the next year to the place where they had first met, and she crossed back over the water the same way.

### The lion

The following year Zosimas went into the desert to find her. In the dry streambed he found her body lying peacefully, with her hands folded and her face turned toward the east. Near her head these words were written in the sand: *"Abba Zosimas, bury the body of humble Mary in this place. Give dust to dust. Pray for me. I died on the first day of April, on the very night of the saving Passion of Christ, after receiving the Divine Mysteries."* So he learned her name, and that she had reached this place, a journey of twenty days, within one hour after receiving Communion, and had died at once.

Zosimas wanted to bury her, but the ground was hard and he had nothing to dig with. Then he saw a huge lion standing beside her body, licking her feet. He was afraid at first, but he remembered her holiness and said: "The great Mary told me to bury her body, but I am old and have no spade. Dig with your claws, so that we may bury her." The lion dug a grave with its paws. Zosimas buried the saint with tears and prayers, and the lion went away into the desert as meekly as a lamb.

[[scene:lion]]

Zosimas returned to his monastery and told the brothers everything, and they glorified God. He lived to nearly a hundred years old.

### The model of repentance

The Church remembers St Mary of Egypt on April 1, and also on the fifth Sunday of Great Lent, so that her life may encourage all of us on the way of repentance. She shows that there is no sin too great for God's mercy, if we turn back to Him with all our heart, and that the Mother of God is a swift helper to those who call on her. Holy mother Mary, pray to Christ God for us!
