### A child wrapped in fire

The holy prophet Elijah was born about nine hundred years before the coming of Christ, in the town of Tishbe in the land of Gilead, east of the Jordan, and so he is called Elijah the Tishbite. He came from the tribe of Levi, the tribe of priests. St Dimitri tells, from the tradition of the Church, that when he was born, his father Sobac saw in a vision shining men who wrapped the infant in fire and gave him flames to eat. When Sobac went to Jerusalem and told the priests, they said: "Do not be afraid. This child will be a dwelling of light, his word will be like fire, and he will judge Israel." His name, Elijah, means "My God is the Lord."

From his youth Elijah gave himself completely to God. He lived simply and often alone in the wilderness, praying and fasting. He wore a rough garment of hair with a leather belt and a mantle of sheepskin, and he burned with zeal for the Lord.

### "There shall be no rain"

In those days Israel was ruled by King Ahab, who did more evil than all the kings before him. He married Jezebel, the daughter of the king of Sidon, a fierce worshipper of the idol Baal, and she led the king and the people into idolatry. Temples and altars of Baal were built everywhere, hundreds of his priests were honored at court, and the prophets of the true God were hunted down and killed.

Elijah went to the king and said: *"As the Lord the God of Israel lives, before whom I stand, there shall be neither dew nor rain these years, except by my word."* And the heavens were shut. For three and a half years not a drop of rain fell, and a terrible famine came upon the land, so that the people might understand that the rain comes not from Baal but from the living God.

### The ravens and the widow

The Lord told Elijah to hide by the brook Cherith, east of the Jordan. "You shall drink from the brook," He said, "and I have commanded the ravens to feed you there." And so it was: every morning and every evening the ravens brought him bread and meat, and he drank from the stream.

[[scene:ravens]]

When the brook dried up, the Lord sent him to the town of Zarephath, near Sidon, in the very land of Jezebel. At the town gate he met a poor widow gathering sticks. "Bring me a little water," he said, "and a morsel of bread." "I have no bread," she answered, "only a handful of flour in a jar and a little oil in a jug. I am gathering sticks to bake it for my son and me, so that we may eat it and die." Elijah said: "Do not fear. Make me a little cake first, and then make something for yourself and your son. For the Lord says: the jar of flour shall not be spent, and the jug of oil shall not fail, until the day the Lord sends rain upon the earth." She did as he said, and for many days the flour and the oil never ran out.

Some time later the widow's son fell ill and died. The mother came to Elijah weeping. He took the boy up to his room, laid him on his bed, stretched himself over the child three times and cried to the Lord: "O Lord my God, let this child's soul come back into him." The Lord heard him, and the boy came back to life. Elijah carried him down to his mother and said, "See, your son lives." And she said: "Now I know that you are a man of God, and that the word of the Lord in your mouth is truth."

### Fire on Mount Carmel

In the third year, the Lord sent Elijah back to Ahab. "Is it you, you troubler of Israel?" said the king. "It is not I who have troubled Israel," Elijah answered, "but you and your father's house, because you have forsaken the Lord and followed the Baals. Now gather all Israel to me at Mount Carmel, with the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal."

When the people had gathered, Elijah said to them: *"How long will you limp between two opinions? If the Lord is God, follow Him; but if Baal, then follow him."* The people said nothing. Then he proposed a test. Two bulls would be prepared and laid on wood, but no fire would be lit. The prophets of Baal would call on their god, and Elijah would call on the name of the Lord. "The God who answers by fire," he said, "He is God." And all the people said, "It is well spoken."

The prophets of Baal went first. From morning until noon they cried, "O Baal, answer us!" and danced around their altar, but there was no voice and no answer. At noon Elijah mocked them: "Cry louder! Surely he is a god — perhaps he is busy, or on a journey, or asleep and must be awakened." They cried louder and cut themselves with knives, as was their custom, until evening, but no one answered.

Then Elijah repaired the broken altar of the Lord with twelve stones, one for each tribe of Israel, and dug a trench around it. He laid the wood and the sacrifice on it and had the people pour four great jars of water over it, three times, until the water ran down and filled the trench. At the hour of the evening sacrifice he came near and prayed: *"O Lord, God of Abraham, Isaac and Israel, let it be known this day that You are God in Israel, and that I am Your servant. Answer me, O Lord, answer me, that this people may know that You, the Lord, are God, and that You have turned their hearts back."*

[[scene:carmel]]

Then the fire of the Lord fell and consumed the sacrifice, the wood, the stones and the dust, and licked up the water in the trench. When the people saw it, they fell on their faces and cried: *"The Lord, He is God! The Lord, He is God!"* The false prophets who had led Israel astray were put to death.

Elijah then climbed to the top of Carmel and bowed down with his face between his knees, praying for rain. Seven times he sent his servant to look toward the sea. The seventh time the servant said, "A little cloud like a man's hand is rising from the sea." Soon the sky grew black with clouds and wind, and there was a great rain.

### The still, small voice

When Queen Jezebel heard what had happened, she swore to kill Elijah. He fled into the wilderness, sat down under a broom tree and asked God to take his life. But an angel touched him and said, "Arise and eat," and there beside him was a cake baked on hot stones and a jar of water. Strengthened by that food, he walked forty days and forty nights to Horeb, the mountain of God, where the Lord had appeared to Moses.

There the Lord told him to stand on the mountain. A great and strong wind tore the mountains and broke the rocks, but the Lord was not in the wind. After the wind came an earthquake, but the Lord was not in the earthquake. After the earthquake a fire, but the Lord was not in the fire. And after the fire came *a still, small voice* — the sound of a gentle breeze. When Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his mantle and went out to meet the Lord, who comforted him and told him that seven thousand in Israel had not bowed the knee to Baal. The Lord also commanded him to anoint Elisha, son of Shaphat, as prophet in his place.

Elijah found Elisha plowing with twelve yoke of oxen, and threw his mantle over him. Elisha left everything, said farewell to his parents, and followed Elijah as his disciple.

### Naboth's vineyard

Later, Ahab coveted the vineyard of a man named Naboth, who would not sell the inheritance of his fathers. Jezebel had Naboth falsely accused and stoned, and Ahab took the vineyard. Then Elijah met the king in the vineyard and announced God's judgment upon him and upon Jezebel. Ahab tore his clothes and humbled himself in fasting, and God had mercy and delayed the punishment. So Elijah boldly called kings to account for their injustice.

### The chariot of fire

When the time came for the Lord to take Elijah up to heaven, he and Elisha walked together from Gilgal to Bethel, to Jericho, and to the Jordan. Three times Elijah asked Elisha to stay behind, and three times Elisha answered: "As the Lord lives, and as your soul lives, I will not leave you." At the Jordan, Elijah rolled up his mantle and struck the water, and it parted, and the two of them crossed on dry ground. "Ask what I shall do for you," Elijah said, "before I am taken from you." "Let a double portion of your spirit be upon me," said Elisha.

As they walked on, talking, suddenly a chariot of fire and horses of fire came between them, and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven. Elisha saw it and cried out: *"My father, my father! The chariot of Israel and its horsemen!"* He saw him no more. Elisha picked up the mantle that had fallen from Elijah, struck the waters of the Jordan with it, and they parted for him too. Then the sons of the prophets said, "The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha."

### He will come again

Elijah did not die, but was taken alive into heaven, like Enoch before him. The Holy Scriptures say that he will come again before the great day of the Lord. He appeared with Moses on Mount Tabor, speaking with Christ at His Transfiguration. The Church teaches that before the Second Coming he will return to preach repentance.

Because he shut the heavens and opened them by his prayer, Orthodox farmers pray to the holy prophet Elijah for rain in time of drought and for good weather at harvest. St Elijah teaches us to be zealous for God, not to limp between God and idols, and to listen for the Lord in the still, small voice of prayer. Holy prophet Elijah, pray to God for us!
