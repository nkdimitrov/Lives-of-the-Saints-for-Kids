### The wise princess of Alexandria

The holy great-martyr Catherine was the daughter of Constus, the ruler of Alexandria in Egypt, in the days of the emperor Maximinus. Alexandria was then the most learned city in the world, with its great library and its famous schools, and Catherine studied in them all. She learned the works of the ancient poets and philosophers, rhetoric and medicine, and many languages, and she spoke so wisely that the most learned men of the city were astonished. She was also very beautiful, and of royal blood.

When she was about eighteen, many noble and rich young men asked to marry her. Her mother and relatives urged her to choose one of them, but Catherine replied: "Find me a bridegroom who is my equal in four things: in nobility, in wealth, in beauty and in wisdom. Only such a one will I marry." Her family despaired, for there was no such man.

### The hermit and the icon

Catherine's mother was secretly a Christian. Not far from the city, in a cave, lived a holy elder, a hermit, who was her spiritual father. She brought her daughter to him for advice. The old man listened to Catherine and said: "I know a young man who surpasses you beyond measure in all these things. His nobility is beyond understanding, for He is the King of kings. His riches fill the whole creation. His beauty is brighter than the sun, and His wisdom made the heavens and the earth."

Catherine asked who this could be. The elder gave her an icon of the Most Holy Theotokos holding the Divine Child, and he said: "This is the Virgin Mother of that Bridegroom. Pray to her with faith tonight, and ask her to show you her Son."

That night Catherine prayed before the icon and fell asleep. In her sleep she saw the Queen of Heaven with the Child in her arms, shining with light. But the Child turned His face away from her. "Do not turn away from me," His Mother said. And the Child answered: "She is dark and ugly to me, for she is still unbaptized, poor and foolish, wrapped in the darkness of paganism."

### Betrothed to Christ

In the morning Catherine hurried back to the elder in tears and told him everything. He began to teach her the Christian faith — about the Holy Trinity, about the creation of the world and the fall of man, about the coming of the Son of God, His life, His Cross and His Resurrection — and then he baptized her.

The next night the Theotokos appeared to her again with the Child, and this time He looked on her with love. "Now she is bright and beautiful," He said, "now she is rich and wise." And He placed a ring on her finger, saying: "I take you as My bride. Keep yourself for Me, and accept no earthly bridegroom." When Catherine awoke, she found the ring on her hand. From that hour she gave her whole heart to Christ.

### Before the emperor

Soon afterwards the emperor Maximinus came to Alexandria for a great pagan festival. He commanded all the people to come to the temples and offer sacrifice to the idols, and the city was filled with the smoke of the sacrifices and the cries of animals. Catherine was grieved to see so many souls perishing. She went to the temple, made her way to the emperor and spoke to him boldly. With wise arguments from the philosophers and from reason itself she showed him that the idols were made by human hands and could not be gods, and she called on him to know the one true God, Creator of all.

The emperor was amazed at her words and her learning, but he could not answer her. So he sent throughout his empire for the most famous philosophers and orators, fifty in all, and promised them great rewards if they could defeat this girl in debate.

[[scene:philosophers]]

The wise men came, proud and confident, and mocked her for daring to argue with them. But Catherine, praying inwardly, spoke with such wisdom about the truth of Christ that they were silenced one after another. At last their leader stood up and said: "Unless we are shown something truer than our gods, we will not oppose this maiden. We cannot answer her; we believe that the God she preaches is the true God." And all fifty declared that they believed in Christ.

In a rage, the emperor ordered them all to be burned. Before they died they grieved that they had not been baptized, but Catherine told them that their own blood and the fire would be their baptism. So they died as martyrs, and when their bodies were taken from the fire, not even the hair of their heads had been burned.

### The empress and the soldiers

Then Maximinus tried to win Catherine by flattery. He promised to make her second only to the empress, to set up her statue in the city and have her honored like a goddess. Catherine answered that she was betrothed to Christ, and that she wanted no glory but His. The emperor had her beaten and locked in prison, ordering that she be given no food. But for twelve days a dove from heaven brought her food, and the Lord Himself appeared and strengthened her.

While the emperor was away from the city, the empress Augusta, who had heard about Catherine, went secretly to the prison by night with Porphyrius, the commander of the army. They found the prison filled with light and Catherine sitting peacefully. She spoke to them of Christ and of the joy of heaven, and both of them believed, together with two hundred soldiers who were with Porphyrius.

### The wheel

When the emperor returned and found Catherine healthier and more beautiful than before, he ordered a terrible machine to be made: wheels set with sharp spikes and blades, which would turn and tear her apart. Catherine was brought out before a great crowd. She prayed to God, and as the wheels began to move, an angel of the Lord struck the machine. It shattered into pieces, and many of the torturers and onlookers were struck down, while Catherine stood unharmed.

Then the empress Augusta came down from the palace and openly rebuked her husband for fighting against God. In his fury the emperor had her put to death. Porphyrius and his two hundred soldiers also confessed Christ before everyone and were beheaded.

### The crown of martyrdom

Finally Maximinus condemned Catherine to death by the sword. She went to the place of execution rejoicing, as a bride goes to her wedding. There she prayed for all who would remember her and call on her name, and she bowed her head to the sword. St Dimitri tells that instead of blood, milk flowed from her wound.

Angels took up her holy body and carried it to the top of Mount Sinai, where God had given the Law to Moses. Centuries later, monks of Sinai found her relics there, fragrant and incorrupt, and brought them to the monastery at the foot of the mountain. That monastery is known to this day as St Catherine's.

### The bride of Christ

St Catherine shows us that true wisdom is to know Christ, and that all learning should lead us to Him. She was not afraid to speak the truth before the powerful, and she loved her Bridegroom more than life itself. Holy great-martyr Catherine, pray to Christ God for us!
