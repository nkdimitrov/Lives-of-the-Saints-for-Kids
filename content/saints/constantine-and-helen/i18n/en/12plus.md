### The son of Constantius and Helen

The holy emperor Constantine was born around the year 272 in the city of Naissus, in the Balkans. His father, Constantius, was a Roman general who later became ruler of the western provinces of the empire. He did not yet know Christ, but he was a just and merciful man who refused to persecute the Christians in his lands. His mother, Helen, was a woman of humble birth from Bithynia, wise and good, who would later become a devout Christian and a saint.

As a young man Constantine was sent to the court of the emperor Diocletian in Nicomedia. There he saw with his own eyes the cruel persecution of the Christians and the courage of the martyrs. Because the other rulers were jealous of him and plotted against his life, he escaped and rode to his father in distant Britain. When Constantius died at York in the year 306, the soldiers proclaimed Constantine emperor in his place.

### "By this sign, conquer"

At that time the empire was divided among several rulers who fought one another. In Rome itself ruled the tyrant Maxentius, who oppressed the people and practiced sorcery. In the year 312 Constantine marched against him with an army much smaller than his enemy's. He was troubled, for he knew that his father's gods could not help him, and he began to pray to the one God whom his father had honored, asking Him to reveal Himself and to help him.

One day at noon, as the army was on the march, Constantine saw in the sky above the sun a great Cross of light, and around it the words: *"By this, conquer"* — in Greek, *En touto nika*. His whole army saw it too and was amazed. That night, as he slept, Christ appeared to him with the same sign and commanded him to make a likeness of it and to carry it before his army as a protection against his enemies.

[[scene:vision]]

In the morning Constantine called goldsmiths and had them make a banner in the shape of the Cross, with the first two letters of the name of Christ — the Chi and the Rho — at the top. This standard, called the *labarum*, was carried at the head of the army, and he had the sign of the Cross painted on his soldiers' shields. On the 28th of October 312 the armies met at the Milvian Bridge over the Tiber. Maxentius's army was routed, and the tyrant himself fell into the river and drowned. Constantine entered Rome in triumph. He would not go up to the temple of the idols to give thanks, as the emperors had always done, but had a statue of himself set up holding a cross, with the inscription: *"By this saving sign I have freed your city from the yoke of the tyrant."*

### Freedom for the Church

The next year, 313, Constantine issued the Edict of Milan, which gave Christians freedom to worship God openly. After almost three hundred years of persecution, the churches that had been seized were returned, the confessors came out of the prisons and the mines, and the exiles came home. Constantine built churches, gave generously to the poor, and honored the bishops as fathers. He abolished crucifixion as a punishment, out of reverence for the Cross of Christ, and made Sunday a day of rest.

But in the East another ruler, Licinius, continued to persecute the Christians; it was under him that the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste suffered. In the year 324 Constantine defeated him and became the sole ruler of the whole Roman Empire.

### The Council of Nicaea

Soon a new danger troubled the Church: the priest Arius of Alexandria taught that the Son of God was a creature and not true God. To bring peace to the Church, Constantine called the bishops of the whole world to the city of Nicaea in the year 325. Three hundred and eighteen holy fathers came, among them St Nicholas of Myra, St Spyridon of Trimythous, and many confessors who still bore the scars of their sufferings. The emperor honored them greatly; he would not sit until they had sat, and he kissed the wounds of those who had suffered for Christ. The Council condemned the teaching of Arius and confessed in the Creed that the Son is *"of one essence with the Father."*

### Helen and the Precious Cross

The holy Empress Helen, the emperor's mother, burned with love for Christ. Though she was nearly eighty years old, she set out for Jerusalem to venerate the places of the Lord's suffering and resurrection and to find the Cross on which He had been crucified.

Jerusalem had been destroyed by the Romans long before and rebuilt as a pagan city. Over Golgotha and the Tomb of the Lord, the emperor Hadrian had heaped up earth and built a temple to the goddess Venus, so that Christians would not gather there to pray. No one knew exactly where the Cross was buried. Helen asked the old men of the city, and at last she learned from a man named Judas — who later became a Christian, and a bishop named Cyriacus — where the place was. Helen had the pagan temple torn down and the earth dug away. Deep in the ground, the diggers found the Tomb of the Lord, and near it three crosses, the nails, and the board on which Pilate had written, *"Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews."*

But which cross was the Lord's? The holy Bishop Macarius of Jerusalem prayed, and then had the crosses brought, one after another, to a woman of the city who was gravely ill and near death. When the first two touched her, nothing happened. But when the third Cross was laid upon her, she rose up healthy. St Dimitri tells that a dead man being carried past for burial was also raised to life when the Cross touched him. So everyone knew that this was the life-giving Cross of Christ.

[[scene:cross]]

Such a crowd came to see the Cross that they could not all come near. So Bishop Macarius stood on a high place and lifted the Cross up for everyone to see, and the people cried out again and again: *"Lord, have mercy!"* The Church remembers this event every year on the 14th of September, the feast of the Elevation of the Precious Cross. The tradition says that sweet-smelling basil grew on the place where the Cross had lain, and so basil is used in church on that feast.

St Helen built a great church over Golgotha and the Holy Sepulchre, the Church of the Resurrection, and others in Bethlehem over the cave of the Nativity, and on the Mount of Olives at the place of the Ascension. She gave alms to the poor, set prisoners free, and once served a meal to the consecrated virgins of Jerusalem with her own hands, as a humble servant. She sent part of the Cross and the nails to her son, and left part of the Cross in Jerusalem. Soon afterwards, about eighty years old, she fell asleep in the Lord.

### The new Rome

Constantine wished to build a new capital for a Christian empire. He chose the old city of Byzantium on the Bosphorus, where Europe meets Asia, and enlarged it greatly. The tradition says that he himself walked out with a spear to mark the line of the new walls, and when his companions said it was too large, he answered: "I will go on until He who walks before me stops." On the 11th of May 330 the new city was solemnly dedicated and given to the protection of the Most Holy Theotokos. It was called Constantinople, the City of Constantine, and the New Rome, and for more than a thousand years it was the heart of the Christian Empire.

### White garments

Near the end of his life Constantine received holy Baptism. The ancient sources tell this in different ways: St Dimitri, following an old tradition, writes that he was baptized in Rome by the holy Pope Sylvester; the historian Eusebius writes that he was baptized near the end of his life at Nicomedia. All agree that after his baptism he would no longer put on the imperial purple, but wore only the white garments of the newly baptized, rejoicing that he had been made worthy of eternal life. He fell asleep in the Lord at Pentecost in the year 337 and was buried in the Church of the Holy Apostles in Constantinople.

### Equal-to-the-Apostles

The Church calls Constantine and Helen *Equal-to-the-Apostles*, because through them the Gospel spread freely throughout the world. They are often shown together on icons, holding the Cross between them. They teach us to see the Cross of Christ as our victory, and to use whatever power God gives us for the good of His Church and of the poor. Holy Constantine and Helen, pray to Christ God for us!
