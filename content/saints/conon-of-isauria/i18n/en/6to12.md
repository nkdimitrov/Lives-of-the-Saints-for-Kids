High in the rocky mountains of Isauria, in the land we now call Turkey, there was a little village called Bidana. Shepherds led their goats along the cliffs, and families grew grapes and olives on the steep slopes. Almost two thousand years ago a boy named Conon was born there. His father was called Nestor and his mother Nada.

### A visit from the Archangel

Conon's parents did not yet know Christ. They prayed to idols, like most people around them. But God had chosen Conon. One day a figure in shining clothes appeared to the boy. It was the Archangel Michael, the commander of God's angels!

The Archangel did not frighten him. Like a kind teacher, he told Conon about the one true God: the Father, who made the world; His Son Jesus Christ, who came down from heaven to save us; and the Holy Spirit, who gives life to everything. Then he baptized Conon in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

[[scene:archangel]]

From that day on, the Archangel Michael stayed close to Conon all his life, like a protector and a big brother, and helped him whenever he was in trouble.

### The candle under the pot

When Conon grew into a young man, his parents arranged for him to marry a girl named Anna. Anna did not know Christ either. On the evening of their wedding, when all the guests had gone home, Conon did something strange. He lit a candle, and then he put a clay pot over it, so the room went dark.

"Anna," he asked, "which is better: light or darkness?"

"Light, of course!" said Anna.

Conon lifted the pot, and the little flame shone again. "The idols are like this pot," he said gently. "They hide the light. But Jesus Christ is the true Light of the world. Whoever follows Him will never walk in darkness."

[[scene:candle]]

They talked late into the night about Jesus. Anna's heart was filled with light, and she believed. Together they decided to give their whole lives to God and to live in purity, loving each other like a brother and a sister. Soon Anna's parents believed too, and then Conon's own father and mother, and Conon baptized them. A whole family had come out of the darkness!

### Stronger than the demons

After his parents and Anna had gone to be with God, Conon spent his days in prayer and fasting. God gave him great power. When he prayed, sick people were healed, and evil spirits fled from the people they had been troubling. The old books even say that the demons had to obey him: he sent some of them to dig the fields and others to guard the orchards, and he shut the rest up in clay jars and buried them under his house!

### The whole town comes running

But the rulers of that time wanted everyone to worship their idols. A governor named Magnus came to Isauria with his soldiers, and Conon was one of the first Christians he arrested.

"Offer a sacrifice to the gods!" Magnus ordered.

"I worship only the living God, Jesus Christ," Conon answered.

So the governor had him beaten cruelly and tied up. But the news ran through the mountains: "They are hurting our Conon!" The people of Isauria grabbed spears, staffs and farm tools and marched out of their town to save him. When the governor's men saw the crowd coming, they ran away as fast as they could!

[[scene:rescue]]

The people found Conon badly hurt but alive, and sick people who came close to him were healed. Conon had been ready to die for Christ, but God gave him two more years with his people. Then he went to the Lord in peace.

### What Conon teaches us

The Church calls St Conon a martyr, because he suffered gladly for Christ. He shows us that one small light can chase away a great darkness, and that God's angels are closer to us than we think. Holy Conon, pray to God for us!
