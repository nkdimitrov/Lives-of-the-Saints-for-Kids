### A village in the mountains

The holy martyr Conon was born in Bidana, a village of Isauria. Isauria was a wild, mountainous land in the Taurus range of southern Asia Minor, famous for its tough and independent people. Its neighbors were Lycaonia and Pamphylia, where the holy Apostle Paul had preached in Lystra, Derbe, Iconium and Perga, and the word of the Gospel had begun to reach the villages in the mountains. Conon's parents, Nestor and Nada, still served the idols. He lived, according to some, in the time of the Apostles themselves; according to others, in the second century.

### The teaching of the Archangel

God chose the boy from his earliest years and gave him a teacher from heaven. The Archangel Michael, the chief commander of the heavenly hosts, appeared to Conon in a bright garment. He taught him the faith of Christ: that there is one God in three Persons, the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit; that the Son of God became man, was crucified and rose from the dead for our salvation; and that the idols are nothing but wood and stone. Then the Archangel baptized him in the name of the holy and life-giving Trinity and gave him the Holy Mysteries of Christ.

From that time the Archangel did not leave him. As the old accounts say, Michael guided him all his life, protected him and helped him in every difficulty, so that Conon grew up wise, pure and fearless.

### The candle under the vessel

When Conon reached manhood, his parents, who did not yet share his faith, wished him to marry, and betrothed him to a maiden named Anna, who was also a pagan. Conon obeyed his parents. But on the first evening, when the wedding guests had gone, he took a lighted candle and placed it under a vessel, so that the room was left in darkness. Then he asked his bride: "Which is better, light or darkness?"

"Light," she replied.

He lifted the vessel from the candle and began to speak to her as a teacher speaks to a beloved disciple. The worship of idols, he said, is like that vessel: it covers the soul and leaves it in darkness. But Christ is the true Light who enlightens every person coming into the world, and whoever follows Him does not walk in darkness but has the light of life. He told her about the Kingdom of Heaven, and about the angelic life of those who keep themselves pure for God.

[[scene:candle]]

Anna's heart was opened. She believed in Christ and was baptized, and she agreed with her husband to remain a virgin. The two lived together all their days as brother and sister, in prayer and good works. Her parents also came to the faith; and Conon, by his words and his holy life, brought his father and mother to Christ as well and baptized them. Some calendars remember his father Nestor among the martyrs.

### Authority over the demons

Not long afterwards Anna and the parents fell asleep in the Lord, and Conon was left alone. He gave himself entirely to fasting, vigil and prayer, living like an ascetic in the midst of the world; for this reason the Greek books call him a *hosiomartyr*, a venerable martyr. God gave him the gift of healing every sickness and great authority over the unclean spirits. He drove the demons out of the possessed, and his name became known throughout the land.

The old Life tells a remarkable story about this. By the power of Christ, Conon commanded the demons as a master commands his servants. Some he made to till the fields, others he set to guard the harvest, and the rest he shut up in clay vessels, which he sealed and buried in the foundations of his house. So the spirits that had once been worshipped as gods were made to labor like slaves, and everyone could see how powerless they are before a servant of Christ.

### Before the governor Magnus

In those days a persecution of the Christians broke out, and by imperial order a governor named Magnus came to Isauria. Conon was among the first to be arrested and brought before him. The governor commanded him to sacrifice to the idols. Conon calmly confessed that he worshipped only the one true God, the Lord Jesus Christ, and would never bow down to demons. Then Magnus had him cruelly beaten, bound and tormented, hoping to break his courage. But the saint endured it all, giving thanks to God, and the Archangel stood invisibly beside him.

### The people take up arms

When the people of Isauria heard what was being done to the holy man who had healed their sick and freed them from evil spirits, they were filled with grief and anger. Taking up whatever weapons they had, they marched out together to defend the martyr. The governor's men, fearing the wrath of the crowd, fled, and the Isaurians found Conon wounded and covered with blood. Those who were sick among them anointed themselves with his blood, and they were healed.

[[scene:rescue]]

Conon had longed to finish his course by dying for the Lord. But God, who knows what is best, granted him two more years of life among his people, so that he might strengthen them in the faith. Then he departed to the Lord in peace, and he was buried near his parents and his wife. The Church honors him as a martyr, because he suffered willingly for Christ, and celebrates his memory on March 5. On the same day she remembers another Conon, the Gardener, a martyr of Pamphylia under the emperor Decius.

### The light that cannot be hidden

St Conon's simple question to his bride, "Which is better, light or darkness?", is a question for every one of us. The light of Christ was given to us at our baptism, and it is easy to cover it with the vessels of laziness, fear or sin. Conon teaches us to lift the vessel off, so that the light may shine for everyone around us; and he reminds us that the holy angels, whom God has sent to guard us, are always near to those who call on Him. Holy martyr Conon, pray to Christ our God for us!
