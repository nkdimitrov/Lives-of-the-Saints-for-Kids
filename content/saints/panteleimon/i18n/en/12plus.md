### The young physician of Nicomedia

The holy great-martyr Panteleimon was born in Nicomedia, a great city of Asia Minor where the emperor Maximian held his court. His father Eustorgius was a wealthy senator and a pagan; his mother Eubula was a Christian, and she taught her little son to pray. But she died while he was still a child, and after that the boy was raised in the ways of the pagans. He was named Pantoleon, which means "a lion in all things."

The boy was gifted and quick to learn. When he had finished his schooling, his father sent him to study medicine with Euphrosynus, the most famous physician in the city, who was the doctor of the emperor himself. Pantoleon soon surpassed all the other students, and the emperor, noticing the handsome and wise young man, wished to keep him at court as his own physician.

### The priest in the hidden house

In those days a great persecution had fallen upon the Christians of Nicomedia; on one Nativity feast thousands of them had been burned in their church. A few survivors hid in the city, among them an old priest named Hermolaus, with two companions, Hermippus and Hermocrates. Hermolaus often saw Pantoleon passing by his window on the way to his teacher's house, and the Spirit of God told him that this young man would become a chosen vessel.

One day Hermolaus called him in and spoke with him gently. "Tell me, young man, what are you studying?" "Medicine," Pantoleon answered. "Your teachers know a great deal," said the old man, "but they can heal only the body, and not always that. Asclepius and the other gods they honor have no power at all. There is a true Physician, the Lord Jesus Christ, who heals both soul and body, who gave sight to the blind and raised the dead by His word. If you believe in Him, you too will heal every sickness in His name." These words went deep into the heart of the young man, for he remembered his mother's faith. From then on he visited Hermolaus every day.

### The child and the viper

One day, walking along a road, Pantoleon saw a child lying dead, bitten by a viper, and the viper still lying beside him. He said to himself: "Now I will see whether the words of Father Hermolaus are true." He looked up to heaven and prayed: "Lord Jesus Christ, if I am to become Your servant, let this child live and this viper die." At once the child rose up alive, and the viper burst apart.

[[scene:child]]

Pantoleon lifted his hands to heaven and gave thanks to Christ. He hurried to Hermolaus, told him everything and asked to be baptized. The old priest baptized him in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and he stayed with him for seven days, rejoicing.

### The blind man and the father

Pantoleon now longed to bring his father to Christ. One day a blind man was brought to their house. He had spent all his money on doctors, but they had only made him worse. Pantoleon said to him: "If you are healed, will you give glory to the God who heals you?" The man promised. Pantoleon touched his eyes and said, "In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, who enlightens the blind, receive your sight." And the man saw. Eustorgius, seeing this miracle, believed in Christ; he smashed the idols in his house and was baptized together with the man who had been healed.

Soon afterwards his father died, and Pantoleon was left with great riches. He set his servants free, gave his wealth to the poor, and visited the sick in their homes and in the prisons, healing all who came to him in the name of Christ, and never taking any payment. For this the Church calls him an *unmercenary* — one who serves without pay.

### Before the emperor

The other physicians of the city were envious, for the sick no longer came to them. They found the man whose sight had been restored and brought him to the emperor as a witness that Pantoleon was a Christian. The man boldly confessed: "It was the God of Pantoleon, Jesus Christ, who opened my eyes." Maximian had him beheaded at once.

Then the emperor summoned Pantoleon. "I have heard bad things about you," he said. "Sacrifice to the gods, and show that the accusations are false." Pantoleon answered: "Let us make a test. Bring a sick man whom the doctors have given up. Let your priests call on their gods, and I will call on my God. Whichever god heals him, let that one be honored." A paralyzed man was carried in. The pagan priests called on Asclepius and the other gods all day, but nothing happened. Then Pantoleon prayed to Christ, took the man by the hand and said, "In the name of Jesus Christ, rise and be well." And the man stood up and walked. Many who saw it believed in Christ, but the pagan priests demanded that Pantoleon be put to death.

### Wonders in the midst of torments

The emperor gave orders that Pantoleon be tortured. They tore his body and burned it with torches; but the Lord appeared to him in the form of Father Hermolaus, saying, "Do not fear, I am with you," and the torches went out and his wounds were healed. They put him in a cauldron of molten lead, but the lead became cool. They threw him into the sea with a stone tied to him, but the stone floated like a leaf and he came back to the shore.

Then the emperor ordered him to be thrown to the wild beasts in the arena. A great crowd gathered to watch. But when the lions, leopards and bears were let loose, they ran to him and lay down at his feet, licking them like gentle lambs, and he blessed them. The people shouted: *"Great is the God of the Christians! Let the righteous young man go free!"* In his fury the emperor had many of those who cried out put to death, and the beasts too.

[[scene:beasts]]

He was bound to a wheel, but the wheel broke. When the emperor demanded to know who had taught him, Pantoleon named Father Hermolaus, and Hermolaus, Hermippus and Hermocrates were brought before the emperor, confessed Christ and were beheaded. The Church remembers them on the day before Panteleimon's feast.

### "All-merciful"

At last the emperor condemned Pantoleon to be beheaded. The soldiers led him outside the city and tied him to an olive tree. As he prayed, one of them struck him with the sword, but the sword bent as if it were made of wax, and he was not harmed. The soldiers were terrified and fell at his feet, believing in Christ, and asked his forgiveness. Then a voice was heard from heaven, calling him no longer Pantoleon but **Panteleimon** — "all-merciful" — for through him the mercy of God would be shown to many.

The saint himself now asked the soldiers to fulfill their orders, so that he might go to his Lord, and they, weeping, kissed him and obeyed. When his head was cut off, milk flowed out with his blood, and the olive tree to which he had been tied was suddenly covered with fruit. This was in the year 305.

### The healer of the sick

St Panteleimon is one of the holy unmercenary physicians, and Christians everywhere pray to him for the sick. His icons show him as a young man holding a little box of medicines and a spoon. He teaches us that every gift we have — our learning, our skill, our strength — is given by God to serve others freely and with love. Holy great-martyr and healer Panteleimon, pray to the merciful God for us!
